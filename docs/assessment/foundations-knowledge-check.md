# Foundations Knowledge Check

Complete this knowledge check before moving to the practical investigation modules.

## Questions

1. What is the difference between publicly available information and intelligence?
2. Why must an investigator define the decision before starting collection?
3. What is the purpose of a priority intelligence requirement?
4. Give two examples of specific information requirements.
5. Why should an investigation include competing hypotheses?
6. What is the difference between source reliability and information credibility?
7. Why does an identical username not prove common ownership?
8. Name three details that should appear in a search log.
9. When should collection stop or be escalated?
10. What distinguishes a fact from an analytical assessment?
11. Why is contradiction-seeking collection necessary?
12. What does proportionality mean in an OSINT investigation?

## Applied questions

### Scenario A

A search result repeats a claim from five different websites. All five sites cite the same original social-media post.

- How many independent sources are present?
- How should the repeated reporting affect confidence?
- What should the investigator seek next?

### Scenario B

An investigator finds a document containing personal information that is not necessary to answer the intelligence requirement.

- What should the investigator do?
- What should be recorded in the investigation log?
- Under what circumstances should the matter be escalated?

### Scenario C

Two accounts use the same handle and profile image but have different creation dates and posting histories.

- List at least four indicators that could help determine whether they are connected.
- Identify one alternative explanation that must remain under consideration.

## Passing standard

A satisfactory submission must:

- answer at least 80% of the questions correctly;
- distinguish source count from source independence;
- demonstrate legal and ethical judgement;
- show understanding of confidence, corroboration and uncertainty.
