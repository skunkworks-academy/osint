# Lab 1 — Validate a Public Claim

## Scenario

A public account claims that a hacktivist group compromised a personal email account linked to a senior law-enforcement leader. The account publishes selected screenshots and documents said to predate the subject’s current office.

Your task is not to retrieve or redistribute breach-derived content. Your task is to assess the credibility of the public claim using lawful, proportionate and reproducible OSINT methods.

## Intelligence requirement

Assess whether the public claim is credible enough to justify escalation for further investigation.

## Required outputs

Submit:

1. an investigation plan;
2. a search log;
3. a source register;
4. a findings matrix;
5. a one-page analytical summary;
6. an appendix listing unresolved questions.

## Constraints

- Use only public and authorised sources.
- Do not download, retain or redistribute sensitive material.
- Do not contact the subject, publisher or associated accounts.
- Do not attempt account access, password testing or active scanning.
- Record any encounter with sensitive content and stop collection where necessary.

## Tasks

### Task 1 — Define hypotheses

Create at least three competing hypotheses about the publisher, the material and the claim.

### Task 2 — Establish the timeline

Identify the earliest observable appearance of the claim, later reposts, corrections, deletions or changes in wording.

### Task 3 — Assess publisher credibility

Review account history, naming consistency, prior claims, linked domains, public statements and evidence of impersonation.

### Task 4 — Validate non-sensitive attributes

Test whether publicly verifiable dates, roles, document formats, locations or organisational details are consistent with reliable records.

### Task 5 — Seek contradiction

Identify evidence that could indicate fabrication, misattribution, recycled material, impersonation or inaccurate chronology.

### Task 6 — Form an assessment

State what is known, what is assessed, what remains unknown and what would change your confidence.

## Assessment rubric

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Scope, legality and proportionality | 20% |
| Planning and intelligence requirements | 15% |
| Search method and documentation | 20% |
| Corroboration and alternative hypotheses | 20% |
| Analytical clarity and confidence | 15% |
| Reporting and evidence traceability | 10% |

## Debrief questions

- Which source most influenced your assessment, and why?
- What evidence appeared persuasive but was weak on closer inspection?
- Which collection action did you deliberately avoid?
- What additional lawful information would most reduce uncertainty?
